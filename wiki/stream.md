# Stream

Messy thoughts, newest first. Random thought, diary, idea, blame, question, nothing in particular — all of it belongs.

Write a heading and dump underneath. Don't tidy it, don't finish it, don't decide if it's good:

```
## 2026-07-17 wine lists are a rip-off

whatever, however long, real line breaks, pipes fine
```

That's your whole job. **I add the kind emoji, the status, and the `Connects:` line** — say *"connect pass"* whenever. Use the **Outline panel** (right sidebar) to scan every entry at a glance.

**Kind:** 💭 random · 📔 diary · 💡 idea · 😤 blame · ❓ question
**Status:** 🌱 raw · 🔗 connected · 🎓 graduated · 🪦 dropped — nothing is ever deleted.

## 2026-09-17 💡 subjects I study secretly talk to each other
🎓 → **Dendrite**, its own vault at `~/Obsidian/dendrite` (see its README)

I have one amazing experience while studying. I didn't recognize it, but when I studied biology, I was amazed by how the microscope was invented for a reason — in order to observe cells or even smaller things. On the other side, when I studied mathematics, I found out that they invented the logic of the integral. And when I studied art, they started to draw with a method of dots. It's not exactly the same era, but they started from similar generations.

A different experience: when I started to study French, I found something weird — July and August don't match counting from one to ten. After that I found out the story is related to Julius Caesar, and that the number seven came from that. So it's not just a language — it's related to history, generations, era, interests, overall.

What I'm thinking: if I keep studying something and save it in the projects, it would be great if some kind of project finds the common things or related points, so I can bond my different studying experiences together — so it increases my experience of studying.

*(added same day)* Those examples are just my personal experiences that inspired the idea. The idea itself is **living logs of my study** — because the more we live, the more we have to study; it's an endless job if you care about personal development. So: living logs, but at the same time increasing the efficiency of studying and bonding different types of study into one connected experience. Another example: math and music sound totally different, but they're actually combined in one huge group — and in the past a lot of mathematicians were also philosophers. Many different subjects are combined into one mainstream, I think.
