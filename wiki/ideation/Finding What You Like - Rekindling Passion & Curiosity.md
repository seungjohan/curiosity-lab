# North Star / Finding What You Like - Rekindling Passion & Curiosity

## Overview
A service for the person who feels **flat** — no strong interests, no passion, no urge to try anything new. Not "which of my dreams should I pursue," but the quieter, more basic problem: *I don't know what I like, and nothing excites me.* North Star helps them **rediscover what they actually enjoy** through gentle, low-pressure exploration — one small spark at a time.

This is deliberately **not** career-focused. The *career* question has its own home: the sibling [[Company Fit Finder - Where to Work|Company Compass]]. North Star sits upstream and more personal — come alive first; the job question comes later, if at all.

Promoted from [[../research/career/Finding-Dream-Services|Finding Dream Services]], but **narrowed**: that research covered "purpose/dream discovery" broadly — this page keeps only the *finding-what-you-like* half and hands the career half to Company Compass.

## The Problem
It's easy to spend life just *chasing the trend* — busy, productive-looking, but with no real sense of what you'd genuinely enjoy. For some people the interest was never found; for others it quietly died.
- **Never found it:** in a narrow frame (e.g. Korea, where "if you're not strongly eager for something, studying is the only answer"), there's no built-in step to discover what you like — so you reach adulthood having never really asked.
- **Lost it:** people *with* jobs and full lives still go numb — they lose interest, feel everything is pointless, and some reach "I don't see a reason to live." This isn't a career problem; a promotion won't fix it.

North Star exists for that flatness: **a way to find what you like when you feel like you like nothing.**

## The Wedge (what existing tools miss)
The self-discovery landscape — Gallup/IKIGAI frameworks, AI coaches, cohort programs (Reforge), psychometrics (Pymetrics) — almost all assume **you already have interests and ambitions to sort, rank, or aim.** They're built for the already-motivated.
- They ask "what's your dream?" to someone who has none — and the blank answer just deepens the shame.
- They jump to goals and plans; but the person isn't stuck on *planning*, they're stuck on *feeling anything*.

The opening: **start from zero interest.** Don't ask what you want to be — help you notice the faint, almost-missed things that still spark a flicker, and make trying one of them nearly free.

## Real-World Precedents
Several cultures already build in **structured time to wander and rediscover what you like** — non-graded, low-stakes, exploration for its own sake. North Star's bet is to give that to people who don't get it by geography, culture, or life-stage. *(Verified 2026-07 — see sources.)*
- **Nordics — [folk high schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_high_school)** (Denmark *folkehøjskole*/*efterskole*, Finland *[kansanopisto](https://en.kansanopistot.fi/frontpage/what-is-a-folk-high-school/)*): residential, **non-graded**, pick-your-own-interest years built around personal growth, not credentials. The purest precedent — you go *to find what you like*. **[86% of Danish students](https://www.afsusa.org/programs/denmark-gap-year/)** take a "sabbatical year" first.
- **Ireland — [Transition Year](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/education/post-primary-education/going-to-post-primary-school/transition-year/)**: an optional year (~age 15–16) that pauses exam-study for exploration, projects, and simply trying things.
- **Germany — [journeyman years / *Walz*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wandering_journeyman)**: after training, craftsmen travel **3 years and 1 day**, forbidden within 50 km of home, to experience "the world at large" — leaving the familiar as a way to find yourself.
- **[AmeriCorps service years](https://www.gapyearassociation.org/blog/taking-an-intentional-gap-year-through-americorps-service/)** & **corporate [sabbaticals](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/benefits-compensation/sabbaticals-solution-to-employee-burnout)**: structured breaks whose stated payoff is *personal reflection and reset* — 98% of AmeriCorps servers cited personal growth; sabbaticals are an explicit burnout remedy (6.7% of employees on one by 2024, double 2019).

*The career-fit precedents — [Waterloo co-op](https://uwaterloo.ca/future-students/co-op/how-co-op-works), rotational graduate programs — belong to the sibling [[Company Fit Finder - Where to Work|Company Compass]], not here.*

**The gap North Star fills:** all of these need the right country, employer, or life-window — and none of them help the person who is *already numb* and can't self-start. North Star is the **personal, always-on, low-stakes version** — the "wander and find what you like" step without needing to be 16, quit your job, or emigrate.

## Directions Being Explored
- **Interest Graph, not purpose ladder** — map the faint signals of what a person leans toward (borrowing [[The Connection]]'s graph approach) and let a *liking* emerge from the pattern, rather than demanding they declare a dream. Could bootstrap from [[Taste Detector]]'s footprint-read to beat the cold start.
- **Near-free micro-trials** — an agent that turns a flicker of interest into a **tiny, low-cost taste** you can try this week (an hour, not a career). Core loop: *notice a spark → try a small thing → did it feel alive?* — evidence over introspection.

## Why AI Changes This Now
- **Questionnaire → companion** ([[ai-as-enabler-not-replacer]]): instead of a one-shot quiz, a patient conversational agent notices faint patterns across weeks of low-friction contact — meeting a numb person where they are, augmenting their self-noticing rather than answering for them.
- **The interpretation is the asset** ([[interpretation-over-artifact]]): what matters is the person's own rekindled *liking* — the chat logs and inputs are just raw material.

## Challenges
- **Cold start on passion**: the target user starts with *near-zero signal* — the hardest possible input. If [[Taste Detector]]-style footprint reading can seed a first guess, that's a real unlock.
- **Mental-health border**: "nothing excites me / no reason to live" edges into depression and languishing. North Star is **not** therapy — it must know its limits, avoid harm, and hand off when needed. Direct tie to [[Been There]].
- **The atrophy trap** ([[abstraction-raises-altitude]], atrophy edge): if the agent hands someone interests instead of helping them *feel* their own, it builds nothing durable.
- **Activation energy**: for a numb person, even a one-hour trial is a high bar — lowering that is the core design problem and the real retention test.
- **Measuring success**: "did they come to like something?" is slow, fuzzy, and deeply personal — hard to instrument honestly.
