> **Why this matters:** Burnout isn't a character flaw — it's triggered by life events everyone eventually hits (first job, layoff, marriage, a new baby, loss), just on different timelines. The people best equipped to advise you aren't credentialed strangers; they're the ones who already survived *your specific* moment.
> **How to use it:** Two loops — (1) check your mental health *regularly*, before a crisis, by letting AI read your life-as-data; (2) when something surfaces, route you to a peer who's already been through it — a "senior" of that exact issue.

# Been There

*(Working title — the idea is the peer who's already been there. Rename freely.)*

## Overview
A space that does two things that reinforce each other:

1. **Regular mental-health check-in.** Not "wait until you break." A preventive pulse you take on a cadence, because burnout arrives *unexpectedly* and its causes shift with age and life stage. AI makes this newly cheap — it's easy now to transfer your life into data and let a system read the drift.
2. **Peer advice from someone who's been there.** When an issue shows up, connect to a person who already faced the same one and came out the other side — advice from a *senior of that specific issue*, not generic wisdom. The platform becomes a **bridge between people**, indexed by lived experience.

The insight that widens it: **you don't have to be in crisis to use it.** Because the unexpected can land anytime, the regular check-in is valuable in an ordinary, healthy life too — it's maintenance, not repair.

## The Two Pillars

### 1. The Check-in (self → data → signal)
- A recurring pulse on how you're actually doing, ideally low-friction.
- "Life as data": with AI, the messy inputs of a life (journal, calendar load, sleep, messages, mood notes — whatever you choose to share) can be read for early drift toward burnout.
- The point is **timing**: catch the slide *before* it becomes a crisis, since people rarely notice their own gradual burnout.

### 2. The Bridge (you → someone who's been there)
- Match a person facing an issue with someone who already lived through *the same* one — new-parent exhaustion, a layoff, a caregiving stretch, an identity wobble after a big life change.
- Value = **specific, earned experience**, not credentials. The advisor's qualification is that they *survived this exact thing*.
- Reframes the product from "another wellness app" to a **human connection layer** — closer to [[The Connection]] than to a chatbot.

## Why Now
- **AI makes life-as-data cheap.** Turning a scattered life into a readable signal used to need a therapist's hour; now a system can do a first-pass read continuously and privately.
- **Everyone hits the same events, off-schedule.** Marriage, first job, job loss, a baby, loss of someone — universal triggers, individual timing. That mismatch is exactly what a large pool of "people who already passed that milestone" solves: someone, somewhere, is always ~5 years ahead of you on your specific thing.

## The Hard Problems (flagged early)
The user named these directly — they're the crux, not footnotes:
- **Who gets the right to advise?** Selecting and *verifying* peer advisors is the central trust problem. Lived experience ≠ ability to advise safely. Bad advice on mental health can do real harm.
- **Safety & liability.** Peer support is not therapy. Where's the line, and how do you escalate someone in genuine crisis to a professional rather than a peer?
- **Privacy of life-as-data.** Reading someone's life to detect burnout is deeply sensitive — needs airtight consent, clear data handling, and probably local/user-owned data. (Same trust bar as [[Taste Detector]].)
- **Matching quality.** "Same issue" is high-dimensional — a layoff at 25 single ≠ a layoff at 45 with kids. Good matching is the moat *and* the risk.
- **Check-in fatigue.** A recurring pulse that feels like a chore gets abandoned. Friction is the enemy (same lesson as [[../stream|Stream]]).
- **Is this even the final shape?** The user is explicit that this is a direction, not a settled solution. The durable core = *regular check-in + advice from someone who's been there*; the packaging is open.
