# Bridging structural holes

The most valuable connection spans the *gap between* two otherwise-disconnected clusters, not the dense interior of one. A "structural hole" (Ronald Burt, 2004) is the empty space between two groups that don't know about each other; whoever **brokers** across it gets a vision of options neither group can see, and that brokerage is where novel ideas disproportionately come from. Adding another link *inside* an already-dense cluster is cheap and low-value; bridging a hole is rare and high-value.

**Test:** does the value here come from *connecting two distant domains that rarely meet* — rather than from deepening or refining a single one?

**Double edge (`axis: bridge-vs-deepen`):** brokering breadth surfaces novelty but stays shallow; deepening one domain builds mastery but goes blind to outside options. A page on the `bridge` side treats the cross-domain link as the asset (this vault's whole premise, 의외의 연결성); a page on the `deepen` side treats depth-in-one-field as the asset. The two are in genuine tension — you cannot maximize both at once.

Cousin to [[multi-signal-fusion]] (which *combines* signals into one decision) — but distinct: fusion pools inputs toward a verdict; bridging spans a gap toward a novel idea. Cousin to [[abstraction-raises-altitude]] — abstraction is often *how* you spot that two distant things share a structure worth bridging.
