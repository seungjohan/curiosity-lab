> [!IMPORTANT] Key Takeaway
> **Why this matters:** A curated list of restaurants provides a high-signal filter for culinary excellence across different styles and regions.
> **How to use it:** Refer to these guides when researching regional cuisines, technique mastery, or product focus (e.g., meat, sustainability).
> **Informs:** Future dining decisions, culinary research, and technique-based cooking inspiration.

# 🍴 Selected Restaurant Sources

This page documents the primary culinary communities and guides referred to for identifying exceptional restaurants globally. The data is periodically updated from these trusted sources.

## 🌟 Primary Communities & Guides

### 1. Michelin Guide
The gold standard for fine dining globally. It focuses on the quality of ingredients, mastery of technique, the personality of the chef in their cuisine, value for money, and consistency.
*   **Focus:** Technical perfection, service, and consistency.
*   **Scoped Data:** 3-Star and 2-Star establishments globally.

### 2. The World's 50 Best Restaurants
A global ranking based on the opinions of over 1,000 international culinary experts. It often highlights more avant-garde and culturally significant restaurants that might not fit the traditional Michelin mold.
*   **Focus:** Innovation, cultural impact, and global trends.
*   **Scoped Data:** Top 100 list.

### 3. Guía Repsol (Spain & Portugal)
The premier culinary guide for the Iberian Peninsula. It provides a more localized and deep-dive perspective on Spanish and Portuguese gastronomy, from traditional "casas de comidas" to avant-garde temples.
*   **Focus:** Iberian excellence, product focus, and regional authenticity.
*   **Scoped Data:** 3 Soles, 2 Soles, and 1 Sol awardees.

### 4. World's 101 Best Steak Restaurants
A specialized guide focused exclusively on the art of meat and grilling. It evaluates the breed, aging, cut, and fire-management of the world's best steakhouses.
*   **Focus:** Product-driven excellence, fire management, and meat aging.
*   **Scoped Data:** Full 101 list.

### 5. Lonely Planet
A globally recognized travel guide that offers curated recommendations based on local expertise, providing insights into authentic and accessible culinary experiences.
*   **Focus:** Local authenticity, street food, and regional culinary culture.
*   **Scoped Data:** Recommended dining destinations.

### 6. Gault & Millau
A French-founded culinary guide focused on technical mastery and innovation, using a 1-20 point scale to rate restaurants.
*   **Focus:** Technical perfection, innovation, and chef creativity.
*   **Scoped Data:** Rated establishments (1-20 scale).

### 7. La Liste
A meta-aggregator that compiles ratings from over 1,000 international guides, publications, and customer review sites.
*   **Focus:** Broad consensus and high-level ranking.
*   **Scoped Data:** Aggregate top rankings.

### 8. Opinionated About Dining (OAD)
A weighted list based on reviews from a community of experienced global diners.
*   **Focus:** Trend-setting, experiential, and avant-garde dining.
*   **Scoped Data:** Global top lists.

### 9. Texas Monthly
The definitive American authority on barbecue, best known for its quadrennial "Top 50 BBQ Joints in Texas" ranking judged by a dedicated barbecue editor.
*   **Focus:** Product-driven excellence in Texas/American barbecue — smoke, bark, brisket, and craft.
*   **Scoped Data:** Top 50 BBQ Joints in Texas.

### 10. 우슐랭 — 우체국 추천 맛집가이드 (Korea Post Recommended Restaurant Guide)
Published annually since 2024 by 부산지방우정청 (Busan Regional Postal Administration), a public branch of Korea Post. Nicknamed "우슐랭" (우체국 + 미슐랭). Restaurants are recommended by **집배원 (mail carriers) and post office staff** — ~4,500 employees across 37 post offices in 부산·울산·경남, over ~5 months. Carriers cover tens of km of alleyways daily, so they know local spots no guide reaches.
*   **Focus:** Genuine local "alley" restaurants at everyday prices — **no advertising, no paid placement, non-commercial** (a public-service project to support neighbourhood commerce). This is the purest *local-verification* signal in this list.
*   **Why it matters here:** it is the anti-Michelin — where Michelin certifies global prestige, 우슐랭 certifies insider local trust at 만원대 prices. Ideal **Tier 3 (Local Verification)** input for [[../../projects/Michelin Filter|Michelin Filter]].
*   **Scope:** 부산·울산·경남 only. 2024: 244 entries · 2025: 245 entries · 2026: 240 entries (published 2026-06-18).
*   **Scraped Data:** [[../../../raw/ushulang_2024_post_office_guide.csv|2024 (244)]] · [[../../../raw/ushulang_2025_post_office_guide.csv|2025 (245)]]. Sources: official PDF via [Korea Post notice](https://www.koreapost.go.kr/user/bbs/638/98/1932/bbsDataView/100081301.do) (2024) and [ushulang.com](https://ushulang.com/) (2025, community-built map of the guidebook). **2026 not yet captured** — distributed via KakaoMap + 부산지방우정청 Naver blog only.
*   ⚠️ **Editions rotate, they don't accumulate:** only 170 restaurants are shared between 2024 and 2025 — 73 appear *only* in 2024. Keep every edition; each year is a distinct dataset.

### 11. AVPN — Associazione Verace Pizza Napoletana (Vera Pizza)
Founded in Naples in 1984, AVPN is not a ranking but a **certification body**: it defines the technical spec for *Vera Pizza Napoletana* and licenses the trademark to pizzerias worldwide that pass an on-site inspection. The spec is unusually concrete — 00 flour / water / salt / yeast only, hand-stretched (no rolling pin), San Marzano-type tomatoes, mozzarella di bufala campana DOP or fior di latte, wood-fired dome oven at ~485 °C, 60–90 second bake, ≤35 cm diameter with a raised *cornicione*.
*   **Focus:** Method authenticity and product provenance — a pass/fail standard, not a score. Related: the *Arte del pizzaiuolo napoletano* was inscribed on the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2017.
*   **Why it matters here:** it is a **different axis** from every other source in this list. Michelin, 50 Best and OAD rank *how good* a restaurant is; AVPN certifies *whether it is the real thing*. That makes it a clean binary signal for a single dish category — useful as a provenance/authenticity input to [[../../projects/Michelin Filter|Michelin Filter]] rather than a quality tier.
*   **Scope:** Global (headquartered in Naples), single-category — Neapolitan pizza only. Membership is a searchable directory of certified pizzerias by country/city.
*   **Scraped Data:** not yet captured — directory at [pizzanapoletana.org](https://www.pizzanapoletana.org/).
*   ⚠️ **Certification ≠ excellence:** membership lapses and is self-selected (pizzerias apply and pay dues), so absence from the list is weak evidence. Treat it as a positive-only signal.

### 12. Guia Boa Cama Boa Mesa (Expresso, Portugal)
Portugal's most established national guide, published annually by the newspaper *Expresso* since 2003 — 23 editions. Created by Expresso founder Francisco Pinto Balsemão, now continued by Francisco Pedro Balsemão. Unlike Michelin's anonymous inspectors or Macarfi's crowd of ambassadors, selection is made by a **team of staff journalists who tour the entire country each year**, including Madeira and the Azores. Covers *both* halves of its name: **Boa Mesa** = restaurants (Garfos/Forks), **Boa Cama** = accommodation (Chaves/Keys), each in Platina / Ouro / Prata tiers.
*   **Focus:** National coverage and consistency over avant-garde ambition. Its Prata tier is where regional, traditional and family houses surface that Michelin never lists.
*   **Why it matters here:** it is the only Portuguese source with a **hotel axis alongside the restaurant axis** — useful for trip planning, not just meal picking. Its tiers are journalist-judged, so it is a *different kind of evidence* from Michelin (inspector) or [[#13. O Melhor Pastel de Nata (Portugal)|Pastel de Nata]] (blind tasting panel).
*   **Scope:** 2026 edition recommends **602 restaurants + 356 accommodations**; awards go to only 39 restaurants and 22 accommodations. 2026 ceremony: 22 April, Terminal de Cruzeiros de Leixões, Matosinhos. Chef do Ano 2026: Vítor Matos (Antiqvvm).
*   **Scraped Data:** [[../../../raw/boa_cama_boa_mesa_2026.csv|2026 awards (63 rows)]] — the full Platina/Ouro/Prata lists for both forks and keys.
*   ⚠️ **The 602-restaurant guide is NOT open data.** `expresso.pt` serves 403 to automated clients and the full listing lives in a **paid print book** (2026 edition on sale 24 April). Only the ~39 award winners are publicly reported, and that is what was captured. Do not expect to scrape the long tail without buying the guide.

### 13. O Melhor Pastel de Nata (Portugal)
A single-product competition, not a guide — the pastry analogue of [[#11. AVPN — Associazione Verace Pizza Napoletana (Vera Pizza)|AVPN]]'s narrow focus, but judged by contest rather than certification. Created in **2009** by Edições do Gosto with gastronome **Virgílio Nogueiro Gomes** as jury president. Entrants are scored by **blind tasting** on five explicit criteria: *aspeto* (appearance), *toque da massa* (pastry texture), *sabor e consistência da massa* (pastry flavour and consistency), *recheio* (filling), and *sabor global* (overall flavour). A shortlist of **12 finalists** contests a public final.
*   **Focus:** One dish, one region — deep rather than broad. The winners are overwhelmingly **neighbourhood pastelarias in the Lisbon suburbs** (Amadora, Damaia, Pontinha, Odivelas, Ericeira), not the tourist-famous Belém houses. That inversion is the signal.
*   **Why it matters here:** it is the cleanest **local-verification** source for Portugal in this list — the same role 우슐랭 plays for 부울경. Blind tasting on published criteria removes the reputation bias that every reputation-based guide carries.
*   **Scope:** Área Metropolitana de Lisboa only. 17th edition in 2026; final on **28 September 2026** at the Jardins do Marquês de Pombal, Oeiras — **result still pending at time of capture**.
*   **Scraped Data:** [[../../../raw/melhor_pastel_de_nata.csv|winners 2009–2025 + 2026 finalists (33 rows)]].
*   ⚠️ **Four gap years — 2014, 2016, 2017, 2018 — are unresolved.** The authoritative `lista-de-premiados` PDF on `melhorpasteldenata.pt` is behind a Cloudflare challenge; the years captured come from press reporting. **2020 appears to have had no edition** (inferred from edition numbering: 2019 = 11th, 2022 = 13th), most likely COVID — this is an inference, not a sourced fact. Pastelaria Alcoa is a known past winner whose year could not be pinned.


### 14. Guía Macarfi (Spain)
Founded 2015 by Manuel Carreras Fisas. **The structural twin of [[#🌏 Local Communities by Country|Blue Ribbon Survey]]** — and the answer to "is there a Blue Ribbon for Spain?". Roughly **2,000 independent local *embajadores*** — ordinary people with food judgment, contributing freely, anonymously and unpaid — score food, décor, service and price; all reviews of one restaurant are synthesised into a single final critique. Covers **~5,000 restaurants**, expanded from 8 to 15 autonomous communities in 2026.
*   **Focus:** Breadth and ordinariness. Not a fine-dining list — it ranks restaurants of every type, and publishes filters that no inspector guide offers: *"where locals eat"*, *"best menú del día"*, *"outstanding bravas"*, *"gems to discover"*, *"rookies"* (best new openings).
*   **Why it matters here:** it is the only Spanish source whose judging body is **distributed and non-professional**. Michelin is inspectors, Repsol is staff, 50 Best is an industry academy — Macarfi is the diners themselves, which makes it genuinely independent evidence rather than a correlated opinion.
*   **Scraped Data:** [[../../../raw/guides/macarfi_spain.csv|1,964 restaurants across 16 provinces]] — names and provinces only; prices and addresses not yet captured.
*   ⚠️ The founder's claim that Macarfi agrees with chefs' own view of their restaurant "90% of the time" is **self-reported**, with no independent validation.

## 💰 The Value Tier — finding "reasonable", not just "good"
Every source above answers *how good*. Only a few answer *how much*, and they are the ones that matter for [[../../projects/Michelin Filter|Michelin Filter]]'s core word, **"reasonable"**.

| Source | Coverage | How it bounds price |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Michelin Bib Gourmand** | [[../../../raw/guides/michelin_bib_gourmand_es_pt.csv|197 ES · 26 PT]] | **A hard cap** — €40 for three courses in Spain. The only enforced ceiling in any guide here. |
| **Guía Repsol Soletes** | ~5,500 ES · 285 PT | Explicitly "casual and affordable"; includes cafés, pastelarias, gelatarias, tascas, quiosques |
| **Prémios Mesa Marcada — Mesa Diária** | [[../../../raw/guides/mesa_marcada_mesa_diaria.csv|PT, 1/year]] | Peer-jury award for *restaurantes de preço moderado* |
| **Guía Macarfi filters** | ES | "Best menú del día", "where locals eat" |

**Two things the price data proved, both counter-intuitive:**

1. **Bib Gourmand is the only tier that actually tracks price.** Measured across 81 Barcelona/Lisboa/Porto restaurants: Bib mean **€45**; Michelin *Selected*, Repsol *1 Sol* and Repsol *2 Soles* all sit at **€70–72**. Award prestige and price are otherwise uncorrelated — Boa Cama Boa Mesa's *Garfo de Prata* runs €30 to €185.
2. **The strongest value lever is not a guide at all — it is the `menú del día` / `menu executivo`.** The Yeatman (2★, Garfo de Platina) serves a **~€40 set lunch against a €290 tasting**. dop: €20 vs €75. OMA: €16 vs €70. Oníric: €26 vs €80. Xerta: €55 vs €115. **A weekday-lunch column would beat every award tier as a value filter** — worth capturing as a first-class field rather than a note buried in `Price_Detail`.
