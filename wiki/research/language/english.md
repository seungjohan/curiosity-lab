> **How to use it:** Reference this definition for context-aware usage in writing and critical discussion.

# English Vocabulary

| Word | Meaning | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Conceit** | **Excessive pride** — an exaggerated, overly high opinion of oneself, one's abilities, or one's appearance. Synonymous with vanity, egotism, or arrogance. | "His conceit made it difficult for him to accept feedback." |
| | **Literary/artistic device** — an elaborate, fanciful, or far-fetched metaphor. Can also refer to the overarching, clever organizing premise of a plot or work. | "The film's central conceit hinges on a time-loop that forces the protagonist to relive the same day." |
| **fizzle out** | **Fading conclusion** — started but loses steam, ending anti-climactically. (KR: **흐지부지되다**) | "My plans keep fizzling out before I finish them." |
| **peter out** | **Gradual weakening** — gradually weakens, diminishes, or tapers off. (KR: **흐지부지되다**) | "The project kept petering out after a strong start." |
| **instill in** | **Gradually introduce** — to gradually but firmly establish an idea, attitude, or habit in a person's mind. (KR: **스며들게 하다 / 심어주다**) | "It is important to instill a love of learning in children." |
| **prestige** | **High status / reputation** — widespread respect and admiration felt for someone or something on the basis of a perception of their achievements or quality. | "The university has a great deal of prestige." |
| **unleash** | **Release powerful force** — cause a strong or violent force to be released or become unrestrained. (KR: **촉발하다 / 해방시키다**) | "The new policy unleashed a wave of protest across the city." |
| **hop on** | **Join quickly** — to join a meeting, call, or vehicle informally and quickly. (KR: **(회의 등에) 잠깐 들어가다 / 합류하다**) | "Can you hop on a quick call to discuss the project?" |
| **back-and-forth** | **Exchange** — an exchange of ideas, arguments, or communication between two or more parties. (KR: **의견 교환 / 실랑이**) | "There was a lot of back-and-forth before we agreed on the final contract." |
| **at scale** | **Broadly applied** — implementing a process, technology, or idea in a way that handles large volumes or a wide user base effectively. (KR: **대규모로**) | "We need to figure out how to make this automated process work at scale." |
| **blow one's mind** | **Overwhelming surprise** — to cause amazement, excitement, or profound shock. (KR: **놀라게 하다 / 감명 깊게 하다**) | "The complexity of this new AI tool really blew my mind." |
| **break free** | **Escape restraint** — to escape from something that holds, confines, or limits you — physical restraint, a bad situation, or a pattern of thinking. Often followed by *from*. (KR: **벗어나다 / 탈피하다**) | "It took him years to break free from the habits he grew up with." |
| **come across** | **Give an impression** — to seem or appear a certain way to others. Often followed by *as*. (KR: **~한 인상을 주다 / ~처럼 보이다**) | "He comes across as confident in interviews." |
| | **Encounter by chance** — to meet, find, or discover something or someone unexpectedly. (KR: **우연히 마주치다 / 발견하다**) | "I came across an old photo while cleaning my desk." |
| **mighty** | **Powerful (adj.)** — possessing great strength, power, or scale; formidable. (KR: **강력한 / 힘센 / 웅장한**) | "The mighty river carved the canyon over millions of years." |
| | **Very (adv., informal)** — extremely or very; a casual intensifier, common in American English. (KR: **매우 / 굉장히**) | "That's mighty kind of you." |
| **guilty pleasure** | **Enjoyable but embarrassing** — something you enjoy despite feeling you shouldn't — often seen as low-brow, uncool, or not good for you — so you enjoy it with a little guilt. (KR: **부끄럽지만 즐기는 것 / 죄책감 드는 즐거움**) | "Reality TV is my guilty pleasure." |
| **bottleneck effect** | **Systems / process** — the single slowest stage in a process caps the throughput of the *whole* process, so optimizing anything else changes nothing until that one constraint is widened. (KR: **병목 현상**) | "Adding more engineers had no bottleneck effect on delivery — code review was the real constraint." |
| | **Population genetics** — a sharp collapse in population size wipes out most genetic diversity, and whichever few individuals happen to survive become the entire future gene pool, so the outcome is set by chance, not fitness. (KR: **병목 효과 / 개체군 병목**) | "The cheetah's lack of genetic diversity is the result of an ancient bottleneck effect." |
